STAR Framework Reference:
- S (Situation): Did they set the context clearly? (Who, What, When, Where)
- T (Task): Did they define their specific responsibility or goal?
- A (Action): Did they describe concrete steps THEY took? (not "we")
- R (Result): Did they share a measurable or observable outcome?

Scoring Guide:
- 9–10 → Complete STAR with specific details, quantified result, strong self-awareness. Impressive.
- 7–8  → Good STAR coverage, minor gaps (e.g., result not quantified). Ready for most companies.
- 5–6  → Partial STAR — story told but missing a key component (usually Result or Task).
- 3–4  → Very vague — generic statements like "I worked hard and it turned out well."
- 1–2  → No real story — only hypothetical or very brief answer with no structure.
- 0    → Off-topic or no attempt.

Evaluate across 6 dimensions (0–10 each):
1. situation_clarity     — Was the context set clearly and specifically? (S)
2. task_definition       — Was their specific role/goal in the situation clear? (T)
3. action_specificity    — Were the concrete actions THEY took described in detail? (A)
4. result_quantification — Was the outcome specific, measurable, or observable? (R)
5. self_awareness        — Do they show reflection, learning, or growth from the experience?
6. communication_structure — Was the story told clearly, concisely, and in a logical order?

Rules:
- Penalize heavily for "we did this" instead of "I did this" in the Action component.
- Penalize for hypothetical answers ("I would...") — this must be a real past experience.
- star_completeness: List exactly which STAR components were present/missing e.g. "S✓ T✓ A✓ R✗"
- improvement_tip: One specific suggestion to improve the STAR structure for next time.
- follow_up_needed = true if the Result (R) was missing or the Action (A) was vague.
- probe_target: Look for ONE concrete, specific detail the candidate actually named — a tool,
  technology, number, teammate, deadline, or decision (e.g. "used Flask", "team of 5",
  "missed the client deadline by two days"). If one exists, phrase it as a single natural
  follow-up question that digs into that exact detail — ideally one that would also surface a
  missing STAR component (Task/Action/Result) through it, the way a real interviewer would
  react to what was just said, e.g. "Why did you choose Flask over FastAPI for that service?"
  or "What did you personally say to the client to manage that two-day slip?". Leave this as ""
  if the answer was too generic/vague to reference anything specific.

strengths (positive indicators) — phrase using concrete evidence from the answer wherever
possible, e.g. "Gave a specific, measurable result (30% faster resolution)" or "Took clear
ownership of the mistake rather than blaming others" or "Reflected on what they'd do
differently" — not generic labels like "Good communication" with nothing to back it up.

red_flags — list each concrete issue with a short reason, or [] if none apply:
- Very short / low-effort answer (say so with the approximate word count)
- Contradicts something the candidate said earlier in this interview, if evident from context
- Answer is irrelevant to the question actually asked
- Buzzwords or generic claims ("team player", "hard worker", "results-driven") with no concrete
  example backing them up
- Unprofessional, offensive, or inappropriate language

'strong leadership skills' but gave no specific instance."
