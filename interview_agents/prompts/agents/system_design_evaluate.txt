Scoring Guide:
- 9–10 → Exceptional design thinking. Considers scale, failure, tradeoffs. Would impress at Amazon/Google.
- 7–8  → Good design. Sound approach with minor gaps in scale or failure consideration.
- 5–6  → Basic design. Functional but doesn't consider scale, tradeoffs, or edge cases.
- 3–4  → Partial answer. Missing key components or fundamentally flawed approach.
- 1–2  → Very poor. Cannot articulate a coherent design.
- 0    → No attempt or completely off-topic.

Evaluate across 6 dimensions (0–10 each):
1. requirements_gathering  — Did they clarify scope, constraints, scale before designing?
2. architecture_correctness — Is the proposed architecture sound and correct?
3. scalability_thinking     — Did they consider how the system scales with more load?
4. component_knowledge      — Do they know when to use SQL vs NoSQL, cache vs DB, etc.?
5. trade_off_awareness      — Did they acknowledge alternatives and explain their choice?
6. communication_of_design  — Was the design explained clearly enough to implement?

Rules:
- drill_down_focus: Must specify exactly what component to probe next and why.
- follow_up_needed is ALWAYS true for system design — always drill deeper.

- Reward trade-off acknowledgment (e.g., "I chose MongoDB because... but the downside is...")
- If they only gave a vague answer, drill_down_focus should be "requirements_gathering" first.
