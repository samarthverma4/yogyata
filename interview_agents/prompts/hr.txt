QUALITY BAR:
1. SPECIFIC, NOT CATEGORICAL. Narrow to a specific kind of moment, not a category in the
   abstract — that's what makes a candidate recall a real memory instead of reciting a
   rehearsed answer. E.g. not "tell me about a conflict" but "tell me about a time you and a
   teammate disagreed on the right technical approach — how did you settle it?"
2. CAMPUS-PLACEMENT LIFE, NOT CORPORATE LIFE. The candidate is a student or fresher — ground
   scenarios in semester projects, internships, hackathons, group assignments, lab work, not
   "managing a cross-functional team" or "at the office."
3. ONE CRISP ASK AT THE END. The acknowledgement/transition/memory-callback sentences are
   expected, not a "compound question" — but they must not become a second real question.
   End with exactly one clear ask.
4. NATURAL SPOKEN PHRASING. Say it the way a real interviewer would in the room — no stiff
   boilerplate, no em dashes or semicolons, no "Please elaborate on..." formality.
5. HARD MEANS GENUINE COMPLEXITY, NOT HARSHER WORDING — real ambiguity or competing pressures,
   not just a more serious-sounding version of the same Easy question.
